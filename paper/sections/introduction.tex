\section{Introduction}

Microsimulation models of tax and transfer policy require individual records
that span domains no single survey covers
\citep{bourguignon2006microsimulation, sutherland2013euromod}. The Current
Population Survey observes employment and income but not wealth; the Survey of
Consumer Finances observes wealth in detail but lacks the \cps{}'s coverage
and program detail; administrative tax records observe income components
precisely but only for filers, and with narrow demographics. Model builders
bridge these gaps with imputation: fit a conditional model of the missing
variables on a donor survey, then draw values for the receiver's records
\citep{little2002statistical, dorazio2006statistical}.

Two properties of this setting distinguish it from generic missing-data
problems. First, both files are \emph{weighted samples}: each record stands in
for a different number of population units, and the quantity of interest is a
population distribution, not a sample one. An imputation model fit to
unweighted records recovers the sample conditional, which differs from the
population conditional exactly when the design (or a prior calibration)
correlates weights with outcomes. Second, the imputed file is not an endpoint.
It is \emph{reweighted downstream}: calibrated to administrative margins
\citep{deville1992calibration, hainmueller2012entropy}, filtered to
subpopulations, stressed under reforms. An imputation that looks
acceptable under the weights it shipped with can therefore fail under weights
it never saw. A rare donor record carrying an extreme value at near-zero weight
barely moves the shipped aggregates; a later reweighting that assigns it mass
turns it into a distortion of the population total. We refer to such records
as landmines. Section~\ref{sec:results-cps} applies a reweighting stress
test for them to a within-survey task (Table~\ref{tab:fragility}), where it
does not separate the weighted fit from the unweighted one.

This paper presents and evaluates the imputation operator of
\populace{},\footnote{\populace{} was first released under the name
Populace. Artifact and release identifiers created before the rename keep
the legacy \texttt{populace} prefix, and the reproducibility pins in the
data-availability section reference the repository at a pre-rename commit.}
\policyengine{}'s open-source microdata stack \citep{microcosm2026}. The
estimator combines three design choices, each aimed at one of the failure
modes above. A \emph{weighted bootstrap} materializes survey weights into
the training data of each forest, so that the forests learn weighted
conditionals. \emph{Regime gates} classify each record's sign class
(negative, zero, positive) before magnitudes are modeled, to keep the mass
structure of zero-inflated and sign-mixed variables, which are common in
economic microdata \citep{mullahy1986specification, lambert1992zero}.
\emph{Sequential chaining} conditions each imputed variable on
the ones already drawn, to carry joint structure among targets through the
imputation \citep{raghunathan2001multivariate, vanbuuren2011mice}. The interface
enforces the first choice: fits are weighted by construction, and an
unweighted fit must be requested explicitly.

We make three contributions:

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{A weight-aware imputation estimator, available standalone.}
We describe the estimator and its interface contract, and release it as
\populacefit{}, installable and usable on plain data frames independently of
the rest of the \populace{} stack.
\item \textbf{The population-view harness.} We frame the evaluation
question as one latent population observed through survey \emph{views}
(variable subsets, designs, measurement idioms), and score any candidate
weighted file by projecting it through each view against that survey's
holdout. The score has four blocks: a strictly proper joint score
\citep{gneiting2007strictly, szekely2013energy}, a support-based coverage
axis that by definition does not depend on the candidate's weights
\citep{naeem2020reliable} (as implemented, up to a weight-proportional
subsample), a weighted classifier two-sample test, and an
uncapped tail block on imputed columns. We added the tail block after
capped sample-geometry metrics failed to register a twofold error in the
99th percentile of imputed net worth. The harness applies to any generator,
and its holdouts appear nowhere upstream of a candidate. Its coverage block
is designed to isolate the part of a file that later calibration cannot
change.
\item \textbf{A benchmark against standard practice with attribution, plus a
reweighting stress test.} We compare the estimator against unweighted and
weighted quantile regression forests \citep{meinshausen2006quantile},
ordinary least squares imputation \citep{vonhippel2013should}, linear
quantile regression \citep{koenker1978regression}, and distance
hot-deck statistical matching, with the donor chosen with and without its
weight \citep{andridge2010review, dorazio2006statistical}, under a paired,
repeated holdout protocol. Ablations knock out one design choice at a time,
and gating and chaining together, so that each choice and the interaction of
those two can be read. A
closed-form fragility diagnostic
measures each method's worst-case single-record exposure under bounded
reweightings of the imputed file (the landmine failure mode).
\end{enumerate}

One policy use shows why the joint matters. Supplemental Security Income
conditions eligibility on countable resources \citep{ssa2024ssi}, and the
\cps{} does not observe wealth. Simulating the program's baseline caseload
on the \cps{}, or a reform such as the SSI Savings Penalty Elimination Act,
which would raise the resource limits \citep{ssi_spea_2025}, therefore
requires imputed wealth, and the composition of the simulated caseload is a
functional of the imputed joint of demographics and wealth. This paper does
not simulate the SSI caseload. It scores the imputed joint of demographics
and wealth, on which such a simulation depends, and it does not score fit
near the resource limits. The
population-view experiment of Section~\ref{sec:task-harness} scores the
imputed joint directly: it asks whether households assembled from \cps{}
demographics and imputed wealth are distributionally exchangeable with
households the \scf{} actually observed.

The rest of the paper proceeds as follows. Section~2 situates the estimator in
the survey-imputation literature. Section~3 specifies the estimator, the
ablation design, the metrics, and the protocol. Section~4 describes the four
benchmark tasks and their data. Section~5 reports results, Section~6
discusses limitations, and Section~7 concludes.
