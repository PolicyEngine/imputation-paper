\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Imputation sits upstream of every population statistic a microsimulation
model produces, and the imputed files are reweighted later by users the
imputer never sees. We presented an estimator whose interface requires the
weights to be stated: a quantile regression forest trained on a resample
drawn by weight, with directly weighted sign gates, sequential chaining, and
an error in place of a silent unweighted fit. We evaluated it with ablations
that switch off one design choice at a time and, in the first application of
the population-view harness \citep{ghenis2026popdgp}, against held-out
\scf{} households on energy distance, coverage, a classifier two-sample
test, and uncapped tail metrics.

Of the three design choices, weighting has the largest effect. On the
\scf{}, whose design is informative, the identical estimator fitted without
weights is 6.1 times worse on net-worth marginal fit on the 2022 wave and
4.3 times on the 2025 wave, and on the population view its imputed 99th
percentile is 1.8 to 2.3 times the holdout's. None of the harness's three
joint metrics captures that inflation. The tail metrics do, and they were
added to the harness for that reason.

Chaining matters where the targets must add up, and there most of its
effect is the adding up. Fitting a balance sheet's components independently
costs 5.8 AUC points at production scale (4.9 on the 2025 wave). Computing
net worth from its components, instead of imputing it, removes that cost,
and chaining the components scores 1.2 points lower still, about half a
standard deviation of the sampling floor.

The gates hold the estimator's zero-share error on zero-inflated components
to about one percentage point. With them off it misses the dividend zero
share by 7 points, or by 4 with chaining off as well. Hot decks and the
unconditional draw, which reuse observed values, hold it within about 2
points without a gate.

Against matching, no method is ahead on every measure. Matching that
chooses donors in proportion to weight equals the estimator on marginal fit
within a survey. On the population view the nearest-neighbour and weighted
random hot decks' AUC is 0.7 to 2.9 points higher than the estimator's on
the 2022 wave, within about one standard deviation of the sampling floor,
and on the 2025 wave the nearest-neighbour hot deck is level with it at
production scale. The estimator's imputed 99th percentile of net worth is 23
to 37 percent below the holdout's across the two waves.

A direct test of conditional fit within each survey, added after the
replication, separates the methods further. On a classifier that tries to
tell imputed from true values given the predictors, the estimator is ahead
of the weight-drawing hot deck on the \scf{} and behind it on the \cps{}. A
constrained hot deck that does not use the weights has the best classifier
score on all three tasks. It gives no donor's record to two receivers, which
the test's four donors for each receiver allow, and with two uses per donor
its score equals the estimator's on the \scf{}. On the \scf{} it also
imputes 1.3 to 2.0 times the
survey's total net worth, where the estimator imputes 0.98 to 1.00 times.
On the population view, where its limit is six uses per donor, its 99th
percentile of net worth is 4.9 to 8.4 times the holdout's.

Marginal metrics rank an unconditional marginal draw first on \scf{} pinball
loss as coded and tied for second of ten methods on the OpenML suite, while
the classifier separates that draw from the holdout at an AUC of 0.968,
against the estimator's 0.761.

The 2025 figures come from a rerun under rules fixed in advance. It
replicated \rep{count:primary:replicated} of the \rep{count:primary}
findings that the abstract or conclusion stated when the rules were frozen
and left \rep{count:primary:inconclusive} inconclusive. Of the
\rep{count:secondary} other findings, \rep{count:secondary:failed} did not
replicate (Appendix~\ref{app:scf2025}). The net-worth-derived variants, the
gates-off ablations, and the weighted random hot deck were added after
those rules were fixed and are outside them. The estimator is available
standalone as \populacefit{}. The task configurations and every table in
this paper regenerate from the committed run files in the accompanying
repository.
