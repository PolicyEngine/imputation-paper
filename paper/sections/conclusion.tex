\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Imputation sits upstream of every population statistic a microsimulation
model produces, and users the imputer never sees reweight the imputed
files. We presented an estimator in which survey weights are part of the
fit and not an option: a weighted bootstrap into the forests, directly
weighted regime gates, sequential chaining, and an interface that refuses a
silent unweighted fit. We also presented a population-view harness, which
scores any candidate population against held-out survey views with a
strictly proper joint score, a support-based coverage axis, a classifier
two-sample test, and an uncapped tail block.

Weighting has the largest single effect in the paper. On the 2022 \scf{},
whose design is informative, the identical estimator fitted without weights
is six times worse on net-worth marginal fit, and on the population view it
inflates the imputed 99th percentile by a factor of 1.9 to 2.3. Capped
joint-geometry metrics do not register that inflation. The tail block does,
and we added it for that reason. Chaining matters where targets are jointly
constrained, and there most of its effect is the constraint: fitting a
balance sheet's components independently costs 5.8 AUC points at production
scale, imposing the accounting identity recovers them, and chaining adds 1.2
more. The gates hold the zero mass of zero-inflated components to about one
percentage point; with them off the same estimator misses the dividend zero
share by 7, and linear quantile regression misses both components by 25 to
27. Matching that chooses donors in proportion to weight equals the
estimator on marginal fit within a survey and scores 1.6 to 2.9 AUC points
behind it on the joint across surveys, about one standard deviation of the
sampling floor. The estimator's imputed 99th percentile of net worth is 23
to 30 percent below the holdout's. Marginal metrics rank an unconditional
marginal draw first on \scf{} pinball loss and tied for second of ten
methods on the OpenML suite, while the classifier separates that draw from
the holdout at an AUC of 0.968, against the estimator's 0.761.

A rerun on the 2025 wave, under rules fixed in advance, replicated
\rep{count:primary:replicated} of the \rep{count:primary} findings that the
abstract or conclusion stated when the rules were frozen and left
\rep{count:primary:inconclusive} inconclusive. Of the \rep{count:secondary}
other findings, \rep{count:secondary:failed} did not replicate
(Appendix~\ref{app:scf2025}). The estimator is available standalone as
\populacefit{}; the harness, task configurations, and every number in this
paper regenerate from the accompanying repository.
