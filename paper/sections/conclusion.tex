\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

Imputation sits upstream of every population statistic a microsimulation
model produces, and users the imputer never sees reweight the imputed
files. We presented an estimator whose interface requires the weights to be
stated: a weighted bootstrap into the forests, directly weighted regime
gates, sequential chaining, and an error in place of a silent unweighted
fit. We also presented a population-view harness, which scores any
candidate population against held-out survey views with a strictly proper
joint score, a support-based coverage axis, a classifier two-sample test,
and an uncapped tail block.

Of the three design choices, weighting has the largest effect. On the
\scf{}, whose design is informative, the identical estimator fitted without
weights is 6.1 times worse on net-worth marginal fit on the 2022 wave and
4.3 times on the 2025 wave, and on the population view its imputed 99th
percentile is 1.8 to 2.3 times the holdout's. None of the harness's three
joint blocks registers that inflation. The tail block does, and we added it
for that reason. Chaining matters where targets are jointly constrained,
and there most of its effect is the constraint: fitting a balance sheet's
components independently costs 5.8 AUC points at production scale (4.9 on
the 2025 wave), imposing the accounting identity recovers them, and
chaining scores 1.2 lower still, about half a standard deviation of the
sampling floor. The gates hold the estimator's zero-share error on
zero-inflated components to about one percentage point; with them off it
misses the dividend zero share by 7 points, or by 4 with chaining off as
well. Hot decks and the unconditional draw, which reuse observed values,
hold it within about 2 points without a gate. Matching that chooses donors
in proportion to weight equals the estimator on marginal fit within a
survey. On the population view the nearest-neighbour and weighted random
hot decks' AUC is 0.7 to 2.9 points higher than the estimator's on the 2022
wave, within about one standard
deviation of the sampling floor, and on the 2025 wave the nearest-neighbour
hot deck is level with it at production scale. The estimator's imputed 99th
percentile of net worth is 23 to 37 percent below the holdout's across the
two waves. Marginal metrics rank an unconditional marginal draw first on
\scf{} pinball loss as coded and tied for second of ten methods on the
OpenML suite, while the classifier separates that draw from the holdout at
an AUC of 0.968, against the estimator's 0.761.

The 2025 figures come from a rerun under rules fixed in advance. It
replicated \rep{count:primary:replicated} of the \rep{count:primary}
findings that the abstract or conclusion stated when the rules were frozen
and left \rep{count:primary:inconclusive} inconclusive. Of the
\rep{count:secondary} other findings, \rep{count:secondary:failed} did not
replicate (Appendix~\ref{app:scf2025}). The net-worth-derived variants, the
gates-off ablations, and the weighted random hot deck were added after
those rules were fixed and are outside them. The estimator is available
standalone as \populacefit{}; the harness, the task configurations, and
every table in this paper regenerate from the committed run files in the
accompanying repository.
