\section*{Abstract}

Microsimulation models impute variables from one survey onto another's
records. A model fitted without design weights learns the sample's
conditional distributions, which differ from the population's where
weights are informative. We present the imputation estimator of
\policyengine{}'s \populace{} microdata stack: a quantile regression forest
with a weighted bootstrap, sign-regime gates, and sequential chaining, whose
interface requires stated weights. To evaluate it we formalize a
\emph{population-view harness}, which scores a candidate file against each
survey's holdout. On two waves of the Survey of Consumer Finances, the
unweighted estimator is 4.3 to 6.1 times worse on net-worth marginal fit and
imputes a 99th percentile 1.8 to 2.3 times the holdout's, which the joint
metrics miss. With its gates off, it
misses the zero share of Current Population Survey dividend income by 7.2
percentage points instead of 0.6 (4.3 with chaining also off). Fitting four
balance-sheet components independently costs 4.9 to 5.8 points of classifier
two-sample AUC, and the accounting identity recovers them. A hot deck that
draws donors by weight matches the estimator on within-survey marginal fit,
and hot decks score within about one noise standard deviation of it on the
joint. The estimator's own imputed 99th percentile is 23 to 37 percent below
the holdout's. Under rules fixed before the second wave was used, six of
eight headline findings replicated and two were inconclusive; five of
nineteen others did not, and the identity and weighted hot deck
arms were added afterwards. The estimator is available standalone as
\populacefit{}.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} imputation, survey weights, quantile regression
forests, statistical matching
