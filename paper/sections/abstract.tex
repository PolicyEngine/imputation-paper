\section*{Abstract}

Microsimulation models impute variables from one survey onto another's
records, and users reweight the result. A model fitted without the
survey's design weights learns the sample's conditional distributions, not
the population's. We present the imputation estimator of \populace{},
\policyengine{}'s open-source microdata stack: a quantile regression forest
with a weighted bootstrap, sign-regime gates, and sequential chaining, whose
interface requires the weights. To evaluate it we formalize a
\emph{population-view harness}, which scores a candidate file against each
survey's holdout through that survey's view of one latent population.
Ablations remove one design choice at a time. On the 2022 Survey of Consumer
Finances, the estimator fitted without weights is six times worse on
net-worth marginal fit and imputes a 99th percentile 1.9 to 2.3 times the
holdout's, which capped joint-geometry metrics miss and an uncapped tail
axis detects. With its gates off, it misses the dividend zero share by 7.2
percentage points instead of 0.6. Fitting four balance-sheet components
independently costs 5.8 points of classifier two-sample AUC; the accounting
identity recovers them, and chaining adds 1.2. A hot deck that draws donors
by weight matches the estimator on within-survey marginal fit and scores 1.6
to 2.9 AUC points behind it across surveys, about one standard deviation of
sampling noise. The estimator's imputed 99th percentile is 23 to 30
percent below the holdout's. A pre-registered rerun on the 2025 wave
replicates six of eight headline findings and ten of nineteen others; five
do not replicate. The estimator is available standalone as \populacefit{}.

\vspace{1em}
\noindent\textbf{Keywords:} imputation, survey weights, quantile regression
forests, statistical matching
