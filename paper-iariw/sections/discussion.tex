\section{Discussion}
\label{sec:discussion}

Read together, the two stages yield an operational recipe.
\emph{Fill with the design}: fit imputation models weighted, gate
zero-inflated components, and chain dependent targets --- the
failures of doing otherwise are invisible to headline joint-geometry
metrics and surface only in the tails and in classifier two-sample
tests, exactly where transfer-program and top-income analysis is
most exposed. \emph{Select with the targets}: treat reduction to a
simulable support as estimation, let the calibration targets drive
record retention, and refit publication weights on the selected
support rather than publishing the gated weights that selected it.
The population-view harness connects the stages: the same latent
population, viewed through surveys for evaluating fill and through
administrative aggregates for evaluating representation.

The limitations are those of each stage plus one that emerges at
the seam. Stage one's results are single-imputation, so reported fit
summarizes one completed dataset rather than an imputation
distribution. Stage two's dense-calibration baseline was still
improving at its optimization budget, so the sparse-versus-dense
comparison is a finite-optimization result on a fixed support, not a
convergence claim. At the seam, certification currently gates the
national and state target families while congressional-district
targets ship as diagnostics; the assignment-only contrast in
\autoref{sec:application} is the measured cost of that gap. Finally,
the companion deployment shows that residual data defects propagate
into named policy provisions (a tip-income carrier undercount halves a
tip-exemption estimate); the discipline the pipeline enables is not
defect-free data but disclosed, provision-level accounting of which
defects bind where.

\subsection{Future work}
\label{sp:sec:future_work}

The next imputation step is to move from single-imputation point
estimates to multiple-imputation inference, and to test how far
imputed tails should extend beyond donor support. Those questions
belong in the population-view harness because the same top-percentile
and classifier diagnostics that reveal weight and chaining failures
can also show whether tail extensions improve the estimand or add
unsupported extrapolation.

For sparse calibration, the matched probe should become a frontier.
The next empirical step is to sweep the normalized sparsity share
across retained counts and run dense no-$L_0$ calibration to an
explicit stopping rule. Holdout panels should remain separate
robustness checks: which target families or geographies generalize
when withheld, which targets dominate the loss, and how sensitive the
result is to the production target weights and cap.

A related thread varies the penalties the headline run deliberately
holds fixed. The reported experiments set $\lambda_{L_2}=0$ and leave
the weight cap off, so the comparison isolates the effect of the
$L_0$ gates on sparsification; this leaves concentration controls as
a separate axis to study. Three experiments follow: compare the
convex $L_1$ selector of Section~\ref{sp:sec:l1_background} with
$L_0$ on the same surface, sweep the soft concentration penalty
$\lambda_{L_2}$ and the hard \texttt{max\_weight\_ratio} cap against
$\lambda_{L_0}$, and use that sweep to trace the
effective-sample-size against accuracy trade-off.

The build-large-then-prune regime is the remaining production stress
test. The current three-year support file is a step toward a pipeline
that assembles a candidate universe far larger than it can ship, then
prunes it to a deployable budget under the calibration targets. More
aggressive support expansion would show whether target-informed
selection adds more when a random draw from a tight budget is less
likely to span the target system. The same production line also needs
to promote congressional-district families from diagnostics into the
certified gate, since the application shows the district-level cost of
assignment-only weights.

The full-surface design also clarifies which classical comparators are
worth adding. Survey-weight, probability-proportional-to-size sampling
is the natural calibrate-then-reduce counterpart to random + reweight
and belongs in the next frontier as a literature comparator. Raking,
GREG, balanced sampling, and combinatorial optimization remain
relevant reference methods, but the cleaner comparison is on simpler
categorical-margin subsets where their assumptions hold cleanly. The
production frontier should continue to use the \populace{} loss over
the complete fiscal surface.
